## 0. How to read this document

| Convention | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MUST / SHALL** | hard requirement; a release failing it is not shippable |
| **SHOULD** | strong default; deviating requires a written justification in §13 |
| **MAY** | optional |
| `FC-n` | Freedom Constraint — governs every other requirement |
| `GOAL-n` | outcome target (what the world looks like when we are done) |
| `REQ-<group>-n` | functional/non-functional requirement, grouped by capability area |
| `AC-<req>` | acceptance criterion — executable or inspectable test of one requirement |
| `OUT-n` | explicitly out of scope, with the reason |

Three rules of construction:

1. **FC constraints are prior.** Every requirement was written to be satisfiable under them; if an
   implementation choice breaks an FC constraint, the requirement is met some other way or dropped —
   the constraint is never relaxed to make a feature convenient.
2. **Every requirement has an acceptance criterion.** If a requirement cannot be inspected or tested,
   it is listed as an open question in §12, not as a requirement.
3. **Current state is cited, not assumed.** "Today" claims reference real files and symbols so a
   reader can verify them in seconds.

## 1. The goal

**Mission (unchanged since v1).** Turn forensic media analysis into a *routed, explainable,
tamper-evident business decision* that any institution can operate at its single verification
choke-point, so that AI-synthesised media cannot silently move money, open accounts, impersonate a
relative on the phone, or get published as fact.

**The v2 goal statement.**

> Any organisation — and any citizen-serving institution that cannot afford to pay — can verify an
> image, audio clip, video, or block of text in seconds, receive a verdict backed by named evidence
> and a recorded rationale, route that verdict into its existing workflow, and prove afterwards what
> was decided and why. **The total licensing cost of doing this is zero, forever: no paid service is
> mandatory, no proprietary component is required, and no model weight used is commercially
> encumbered.**

`GOAL-1` **Latency.** A single 8 MiB item returns a verdict end-to-end (ingest → routed action) in
≤ 2 s on commodity hardware for the heuristic path, and ≤ 8 s including one ML plugin.

`GOAL-2` **Verdict quality.** Against analyst adjudication on a continuously growing labelled corpus:
precision of `BLOCK`/`ESCALATE` ≥ 90 %; `BLOCK` false-positive rate ≤ 0.5 %. Calibration is measured,
not asserted (see §4.1).

`GOAL-3` **Universal availability of the capability.** Every feature in this spec is deployable from
source on free infrastructure in ≤ 30 minutes, with no vendor account, credit card, or licence key
anywhere in the procedure.

`GOAL-4` **Accountability.** 100 % of verdicts are reconstructible from the audit chain plus the
stored report; zero automated actions exist without an `action_rationale`.

`GOAL-5` **No automation of refusal.** Where evidence is thin the system says so. The engine's
`NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW` path (today: `synthverify/xai.py::_route` on low coverage/confidence) is a
first-class product outcome, not an error state — and it is *mandatory* on the free public tier
(§4.6).

`GOAL-6` **Societal reach.** At least three public-interest deployments (newsroom verification desk,
elder-fraud helpline, and one civic-election monitor) operate the full pipeline at zero licence cost
and publish a transparency report generated by the system itself.

## 2. The Freedom Constraint (governs everything)

"Free" has two distinct meanings in this project and **both** are binding.

### 2.1 Free as in zero licensing cost (FOSS supply side)

`FC-1` **Permissive-only dependency policy.** Every runtime and build-time dependency MUST be
licensed under an allowlist: MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD-2/3, ISC, PSF-2.0, LGPL-2.1-or-later *(only where
dynamically linked and never required to modify)*, and MPL-2.0. GPL/AGPL/SSPL/BSL/"source-available"
and non-commercial licences MUST NOT become runtime dependencies of the core product.
*Today: core deps are already MIT/BSD-family (`pyproject.toml`).*

`FC-2` **No mandatory paid service.** Any capability required to run the pipeline — database, queue,
object storage, identity provider, model runtime, observability — MUST have a self-hostable
free/OSS implementation that is the *default*, not the alternative. Cloud-managed equivalents MAY be
supported, MUST NOT be required.

`FC-3` **Open weights or nothing.** Every ML model shipped or recommended MUST provide: (a) weights
under an FC-1-allowlisted licence that permits commercial use, (b) the training recipe or a citation
to a permissively licensed dataset, and (c) a model card with measured error rates per demographic
group. **Research-only or "non-commercial" weights, and models trained on datasets that forbid
commercial use, are disqualified** — this is the most common way "free" forensics tooling turns out
not to be free.
`AC-FC-3` A CI job fails if any registered model lacks a machine-readable
`synthverify/models/*.json` manifest with `license`, `weights_sha256`, `dataset_license`, and
`eval_report` fields whose licences are on the FC-1 allowlist.

`FC-4` **No phone-home, no telemetry, offline-capable.** After install, the pipeline MUST complete a
full ingest→verdict cycle with no outbound network access. Detector calibration updates, model
downloads, and licence checks are operator-initiated only.
`AC-FC-4` CI runs the e2e suite inside a network-isolated container (`NetworkDisabled: true`) and it
passes.

`FC-5` **No per-seat, per-request, or per-organisation fee can be structurally required.** Anything
metered by a third party MUST NOT sit on the verdict path. Webhook delivery, audit storage, and
detector inference MUST be operator-hosted.

`FC-6` **Freedom to fork and operate.** The product MUST be deployable from a source checkout plus
`make setup && make run` with no build step that requires a non-redistributable artefact. All
default model weights MUST be obtainable from a public, non-gated URL or committed to a
redistributable release asset.

`FC-7` **No vendor lock-in in data.** Every persisted artefact (DB rows, media, artifacts, audit
chain, reports) MUST be exportable with documented OSS tools only. Reports are plain JSON under a
versioned schema key (`synthverify.report/v1` today); the audit chain is plain SHA-256 over
canonical JSON.

### 2.2 Free at the point of use (demand side)

`FC-8` **Public-interest tier is free and real.** Newsrooms, public broadcasters, elder-protection and
anti-fraud helplines, election-integrity monitors, libraries, archives, and academic forensics groups
MUST be able to obtain a deployment that is free of licence fees and functionally complete. This is
not a trial: no feature may be paywalled behind a tier.

`FC-9` **Capability must not depend on the operator's ability to pay.** Where an institution cannot
run its own infrastructure, a community-hosted instance MUST offer the same detectors and the same
XAI report — with the *public* tier's routing restricted to advisory (`PROCEED`/`MANUAL_REVIEW`) as
the abuse control, never a degraded model (`OUT-2`).

`FC-10` **Explainability is free too.** Reports must remain human-readable and exportable by the
recipient of a verdict, including a citizen whose photo was judged. The flag glossary, evidence, and
policy thresholds used MUST be obtainable by that person without an account (`GET /api/v1/meta`
today, extended per REQ-XAI-3).

`FC-11` **Accessibility cost is not passed on.** UI localisation and the citizen reporting path
(REQ-REACH-2/3) MUST be implementable with FOSS tooling only, and the UI MUST meet WCAG 2.2 AA.

**The core tension, stated plainly.** Free public verification is an abuse magnet (scammers verifying
their own pretexts; adversaries probing thresholds for evasion). The resolution this spec adopts is
*routing restriction and disclosure, not capability restriction*: the free tier gets the same
detectors but cannot auto-`BLOCK`, and threshold values are public. `AC-FC-9` Any request whose key
carries the public tier is rejected with `422 policy_restriction` if it asks the engine to route to
`BLOCK`, and the response body states why and how to run self-hosted.

## 4. Requirements by capability area

### 4.1 Detection quality — trained models, without selling the licence

Today: 11 heuristic detectors registered via `synthverify/detectors/registry.py::register`; no ML
detector exists; `metadata` detects C2PA/JUMBF *markers* but validates no signature; text has
stylometry only.

`REQ-DET-1` ML detector plugins MUST be implemented as ordinary `Detector` subclasses, using the
existing `DetectionContext` lazy-decode contract, with `weight > 1.0`, and MUST emit findings +
`evidence` in the same XAI shape the heuristics do — a model score alone is not a report.
`AC-DET-1` `tests/test_detectors.py` gains per-model tests on the deterministic corpus in
`tests/fixtures_gen.py`, asserting both discrimination (authentic vs synthetic separation) and that
every non-empty finding string is backed by a numeric field in `evidence`.

`REQ-DET-2` Three model plugins are in scope for v2: face/image deepfake, audio clone/TTS, video
temporal-artefact. Each MUST satisfy FC-3 end-to-end, and MUST run CPU-only by default (GPU is an
accelerator, never a requirement — FC-2).

`REQ-DET-3` **Calibration gates in CI.** Each model ships with an equal-error-rate point, AUC, and
expected-calibration error on a held-out permissively licensed set; CI MUST fail if any metric
regresses beyond the tolerance recorded in the model manifest.
`AC-DET-3` `pytest -m calibration` compares measured metrics against `eval_report` tolerances; gate
values are committed and every change is explained in the diff.

`REQ-DET-4` Uncertainty honesty: a model that cannot assess an input MUST return
`ResultStatus.SKIPPED` with a reason, which lowers `coverage` and can flip the verdict to
`NEEDS_HUMAN_REVIEW`. Silent zero-scores are prohibited.
`AC-DET-4` A test feeds a cropped/low-resolution face and asserts `SKIPPED`, and that the fused
report's `conclusive` is `false`.

`REQ-DET-5` **C2PA / cryptographic provenance validation**, not marker detection: full JUMBF/claim
walk, signature verification using `cryptography` (Apache-2.0/PSF — FC-1 clean), certificate trust
state, and a distinct verdict: `authentic-provenance` / `provenance-invalid` / `provenance-stripped`.
`AC-DET-5` Fixtures include a validly signed, a tampered-after-signing, and a strip-metadata manifest;
the tampered one MUST produce the `provenance-invalid` flag and MUST NOT produce
`CAMERA_ORIGIN_DECLARED`.

`REQ-DET-6` Face-region awareness: landmark-consistency and lip-sync (A/V) checks operating on
detector-shared frame decode.
`REQ-DET-7` Text: add a perplexity-proxy detector using an FC-3-clean open LM, with multilingual
coverage for at least the six UN languages, each locally runnable.
`REQ-DET-8` Known-synthetic fingerprint registry (consortium blocklist) MUST be a locally mirrored,
operator-populated dataset. Syncing to a third-party hosted registry is a *plugin*, off by default, so
FC-4 (offline) and FC-5 survive.

### 4.2 Explainability and routing

`REQ-XAI-1` Every detector's contribution remains derivable in the report. ML plugins MUST NOT change
the fusion math (`Σ(wᵢ·sᵢ·cᵢ)/Σ(wᵢ·cᵢ)` with strong-evidence override) without a versioned
`schema_version` bump.
`REQ-XAI-2` Analyst feedback loop: an overturned verdict is recorded (`decision.overridden` audit
event, org-scoped) and exportable as a labelled training signal, so detection improves without buying
data.
`AC-XAI-2` A test posts an override, then asserts the audit event, the chain still verifies, and the
override appears in `GET /api/v1/admin/feedback`.
`REQ-XAI-3` **Recipient-facing disclosure artefact**: a compact, machine-stable "why this was flagged"
document per verdict, retrievable by a verifier-issued reference token with no account (FC-10), and
translatable with OSS tooling.
`REQ-XAI-4` Per-org policy profiles (done in v1.1: `PolicyProfile` resolution through
`orchestator.resolve_policy`, `policy_name` provenance) MUST be versioned: changing a threshold
records before/after in the ledger so a decision can be replayed under the policy in force at the
time. `AC-XAI-4` Replaying a stored report against its recorded `policy_name` reproduces the identical
recommended action.

### 4.3 Production infrastructure — on free software only

| Area | Today | v2 requirement | Freedom basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Database | SQLite via `Database.create_all()` | `REQ-INFRA-1` PostgreSQL reference deployment **and** Alembic migrations from the first schema change | Postgres = ODbL-family, self-host |
| Queue | in-process `WorkerFleet`, priority queue, crash recovery re-enqueues | `REQ-INFRA-2` swap-in external broker using `process_job(db, id)` as the unit of work; embedded fleet stays the default | Redis (BSD), RabbitMQ (MPL), or Postgres `SKIP LOCKED` — **no paid broker**, and the Postgres-only option must be first-class since many operators already run it |
| Rate limits | in-process token bucket, `RateLimiter.check()` | `REQ-INFRA-3` shared bucket behind the identical `check(subject) -> (allowed, retry_after)` contract — **Valkey** (BSD-3), *not* Redis ≥ 7.4, whose RSALv2/SSPL tri-licence FC-1 forbids (§13, §6.1 interpretation 11) | same `check()` signature is the FC-2 escape hatch: no interface churn |
| Media storage | **no abstraction** — raw FS writes in `routes_media._save_and_create_job`, reads in `worker._load_media` | `REQ-INFRA-4` introduce a `MediaStore` interface (put/get/exists/delete/presign-optional) with local-FS and S3-compatible backends | default backend = **MinIO/S3-compatible self-host**, so "S3-compatible" never means "pay AWS" |
| Retention | none | `REQ-INFRA-5` per-org retention/TTL policy, enforced by a scheduler, with legal-hold pinning for audit | compliance without buying a data-lake product |
| Observability | Prometheus text `/metrics`, `/healthz`, `/readyz` | `REQ-INFRA-6` exemplars + trace propagation using OSS only; alert rules shipped as a file | Prometheus (Apache) — no metered APM on the verdict path (FC-5) |

`AC-INFRA-1` CI matrix runs the whole suite against Postgres and asserts `alembic upgrade head`
produces a schema identical to `create_all()` on a fresh DB (compared via metadata diff).
`AC-INFRA-2` A two-replica docker-compose test (Postgres + Redis, no embedded worker) ingests 200 jobs
and every job completes exactly once, with idempotency preserved.
`AC-INFRA-3` The limiter swap is invisible to its callers: with the backend changed from the
in-process token bucket to the shared one, `check(subject) -> (allowed, retry_after)` keeps its exact
signature and semantics, and (a) two separate processes pointed at the same shared backend admit
**fewer** combined requests than two isolated in-process buckets do — the budget is genuinely global,
(b) an unreachable shared backend degrades to the in-process bucket rather than erroring or hanging,
so `AC-FC-4`'s offline claim survives the swap, and (c) no route handler changes line.
`AC-INFRA-4` Swapping `SV_MEDIA_STORE=local` → `s3` (pointed at MinIO) requires no code change and
passes `tests/test_media_store.py` unchanged.
`AC-INFRA-5` A retention test: with a per-org TTL configured, past-due assets and their jobs are swept
by the scheduler while (a) a legal-hold pin on an audit-relevant resource blocks its deletion and is
itself recorded in the ledger, and (b) the sweep is idempotent — running it twice deletes nothing
twice and reports the same set.
`AC-INFRA-6` A request's trace id appears in its `/metrics` exemplars, in its job's audit ledger row,
and in the log line its worker emits; the alert rules ship as a file in the repo and parse, and no
element of that path requires a metered vendor (FC-5).

### 4.4 Identity, tenancy, integrity

`REQ-IDAM-1` JWT/OIDC *alongside* `sv_live_…` keys for human SSO, validated against an operator-run
OIDC provider (Keycloak, Apache, self-host) with no required paid IdP. Roles map onto the existing
`admin/analyst/service` model rather than inventing a parallel one.
`AC-IDAM-1` A test authenticates a request with a locally minted JWT signed by a test-key Jwks, and
`require_analyst` admits it; a token for another audience is rejected.
`REQ-IDAM-2` Fine-grained scopes (`media:submit`, `jobs:read`, `admin:policy:write`, `artifacts:read`)
so a newsroom can give a contractor read-only access to their own org.
`AC-IDAM-2` A scope matrix test: a credential minted with `jobs:read` + `media:submit` is admitted to
ingest and job-read routes and rejected on `admin:policy:write` and `artifacts:read` with `403` (the
credential is valid and known, so existence is not being leaked — unlike cross-org reads, which stay
`404` per `AC-IDAM-3`), the rejection names the missing scope, and no scope combination grants a read
of another organisation's resource. Keys that predate scopes keep working with their existing
role-equivalent grant, asserted by the same table.
`REQ-IDAM-3` Tenant isolation is *proven*, not assumed: cross-org reads stay `404` on every new
endpoint (already the rule for `/jobs/{id}/artifacts`).
`AC-IDAM-3` A table-driven test enumerates all GET/POST routes with an org-B key against org-A
resources and asserts `404` (never `403`, which leaks existence).
`REQ-IDAM-4` The hash-chained ledger remains append-only under scale: chain verification at
`GET /admin/audit/verify` MUST complete on ≥ 1 M events with a checkpoint scheme, still verifying
every hash between checkpoints.
`AC-IDAM-4` Benchmark proves chain verify of 1 M events < 60 s single-threaded, and a
checkpoint-insertion test detects tampering inside a checkpointed range.

### 4.5 Operator experience

`REQ-OPS-1` Artifact rendering and queue filters (done: `/jobs/{id}/artifacts[/{index}]` with
digest-derived filename re-derivation; blob-URL authenticated display) MUST extend to per-detector
artifacts from the new ML and video detectors (attention/saliency maps, frame diffs), same access
rules.
`REQ-OPS-2` Case workflow: analyst queue with assignment, notes, bulk re-analysis, and override
(REQ-XAI-2) surfaced in the dashboard.
`REQ-OPS-3` Policy editor with a live "what would change" preview using
`GET /admin/policy/effective?organisation=` plus a replay of the org's last N jobs under the new
thresholds — so no policy goes live blind.
`AC-OPS-3` Editing thresholds in the UI lists, for the last 50 jobs, which actions would flip, and
each flip is reproducible by API replay.
`REQ-OPS-4` Single-file dashboard posture (no build step, no CDN) is retained deliberately: it keeps
FC-6 true for anyone air-gapping the console.

### 4.6 Free public access, newsrooms, citizens

`REQ-PUBLIC-1` **Public-interest registration**: a documented path to obtain a full-featured, zero-fee
deployment (FC-8) — self-host, or a community-hosted instance. Eligibility is stated in writing in the
repo; the grant process MUST NOT require paying for anything (including a domain or a managed DB).
`REQ-PUBLIC-2` **Advisory public tier**: an unauthenticated or low-quota tier for citizens/journalists
that returns the full XAI report but routes only to `PROCEED`/`MANUAL_REVIEW` (FC-9) and is
rate-limited per IP with `429`+`Retry-After` (reuse `rate_limit()`'s anonymous branch, which already
keys on client host).
`AC-PUBLIC-2` A public-tier request asking for `BLOCK` routing is refused with the documented
`422 policy_restriction`, and the same media verified on a self-hosted instance yields the identical
score and flags — proving capability parity (FC-9).
`REQ-PUBLIC-3` **Publishable verification result**: a signed, shareable, offline-verifiable statement
("this file, sha256 X, was assessed at T under policy P with action A") a newsroom can attach to an
article, and any reader can independently re-hash and check against the issuer's public key. Uses
COSE/JWS from OSS libs only.
`AC-PUBLIC-3` Given a published attestation and the original file, a third party with no access to the
issuer can verify authenticity and detect file substitution.
`REQ-PUBLIC-4` **Transparency reports generated from the ledger**: aggregates (volume by media type,
action distribution, tier distribution, override rate, detector coverage) computed from the audit
chain, exportable as JSON + static HTML, with no raw media or PII in them.
`REQ-REACH-2` Browser extension for citizen reporting: MUST be FOSS, MUST NOT route media through a
third-party proxy by default (points at the user's chosen instance — FC-4/FC-7), and MUST be
installable from a free browser store or as an unpacked build.
`REQ-REACH-3` Localisation of narratives and the flag glossary into ≥ 6 languages using OSS
translation, with human-review instructions; strings move out of `xai.py` literals into a
resource catalogue.

### 4.7 Real-time verification (the hardest free-tier problem)

`REQ-RT-1` Call-center streaming voice check: a session that consumes live audio (WebRTC/SIP media or
file chunks), runs the audio detectors incrementally, and returns a provisional risk with a staleness
indicator — all on CPU, self-hosted, no metered API (FC-2/FC-5).
`AC-RT-1` A test streams a synthetic-voice fixture over the streaming interface and obtains a verdict
with end-to-end added latency ≤ 800 ms at 1× speed, offline.
`REQ-RT-2` Because live calls are where the elderly are actually harmed, the streaming path MUST expose
the same "refuse to conclude" behaviour (GOAL-5): provisional unknown is surfaced rather than a
comforting low score.
`REQ-RT-3` Out-of-band fallback: helpline staff can verify a recorded voicemail with the same report a
bank gets — no feature is premium here (FC-8).

### 6.1 Status after M1 (freedom gates + the two infrastructure seams), 2026-09-26

Landed and verified, with the exact command in each note:

* **REQ-INFRA-1** — Alembic owns the schema. `synthverify db-upgrade` / `make migrate`;
  `upgrade head ≡ create_all()` is asserted by `compare_metadata` (with `compare_type`) *and* by
  comparing stored DDL, on SQLite and on Postgres 16 — both locally (`SV_TEST_POSTGRES_URL`,
  `make postgres-e2e`) and in CI, where `tests/test_migrations.py` is now exercised by *both* legs of the
  `test` matrix. Revisions are guarded on inspection so a pre-Alembic install converges
  instead of crashing.
* **AC-INFRA-1's other clause — the whole suite on Postgres** — `tests/conftest.py` exposes one seam,
  `new_database_url()`, so *every* fixture-built database (the API app, the CLI subprocesses, the live-uvicorn
  SDK run, the socket-guarded offline cases) becomes a private per-test database on a real server when
  `SV_TEST_POSTGRES_URL` is set, and the ordinary SQLite file when it is not. **422 passed / 0 skipped** on
  Postgres against the same 422 with 14 visible skips on defaults. No test body gained a dialect branch.
* **REQ-INFRA-4** — `MediaStore` with local + S3-compatible backends, selected by `SV_MEDIA_STORE`
  alone; SigV4 on stdlib `hmac`/`hashlib`, so no new runtime dependency (FC-1 stays clean at 45

Interpretations adopted where the text left a choice (recorded here so a future reader sees a
decision, not a drift):

1. **FC-3 "committed gates" are required per measured metric.** A metric inside a plausible range
   proves nothing, so `gates.auc`/`eer`/`ece` must be declared and the measured value must satisfy
   them; an `auc` gate at or below 0.5 is rejected as a gate that cannot fail.
2. **Dataset licences are accepted as data licences**, not software ones: `CC0-1.0` and
   `CDLA-Permissive-2.0` join the allowlist for `dataset_license` only. `CC-BY-4.0` was *not* added -
   attribution obligations on every derived corpus are a real cost, so the shipped template uses CC0.
3. **Ambiguous licence ids resolve to the most restrictive reading** (`bsd` → BSD-3-Clause,
   `gpl` → GPL-3.0-or-later), because a guess must fail closed.
4. **`pg8000` (BSD) is a verification-only test driver** (CI's `test` Postgres leg and `migrations` job and
   `make postgres-e2e`), never a product dependency; production Postgres stays
   on psycopg. The FC-1 scan covers declared dependencies, and keeping an LGPL driver out of even the
   test matrix avoids normalising "we tolerate copyleft for convenience".
5. **`AC-FC-4`'s `NetworkDisabled: true` is read as "no network namespace",** which on Linux docker is
   `--network none`. The suite runs the *analysis*, not the whole e2e file suite, under the seal: an
   e2e test needs a server, and a server with no interface cannot be reached from the test process —
   so the claim under test is FC-4's actual wording ("complete a full ingest→verdict cycle with no
   outbound network access"), proven by `cli analyze` on a real file. The seal is verified before it is
   trusted (`socket.create_connection` to a public address must fail inside, and must succeed outside).
6. **`AC-INFRA-1`'s "CI matrix" is satisfied by the matrix *definition* plus a local run of the same
   command**, because this working copy has no remote and nothing has ever been pushed. The Postgres leg is
   therefore executed here, against a real `postgres:16`, with its anti-degradation and cleanup gates run
   verbatim from the workflow file — but "the hosted runner turned green" is a sentence this repo cannot
   say, and does not say. Reading the clause as "must be proven by GitHub's hosted runners specifically"
   would leave `AC-INFRA-1` open on an infrastructure fact rather than an engineering one, so the wording
   adopted is: matrix defined and committed, whole suite green on both dialects locally, hosted execution
   pending a push.
7. **Every `REQ-*` in §4.3/§4.4 now has an `AC-*` partner, because a requirement without one cannot be
   closed.** §4.3 listed `REQ-INFRA-1..6` but defined only `AC-INFRA-1/2/4`, and §7's condition 3 then
   cited "AC-INFRA-1..4" — a range that named two ids that did not exist (`AC-INFRA-3`), skipped two
   requirements that did (`REQ-INFRA-5/6`), and would have let v2 be declared done with no shared rate
   limiter, no retention sweep and no trace correlation. `AC-INFRA-3/5/6` and `AC-IDAM-2` are now
   specified, and condition 3 reads `1..6`. **This widens the definition of done rather than narrowing
   it**, so §13 carries it explicitly: the widening is the correction, and the alternative — leaving four
   requirements testable by nobody — was the worse edit.
   Measured at the same time, and *not* fixed here: 15 requirements in other sections still carry their
   acceptance clause in the requirement prose instead of a numbered `AC-*` id (`REQ-DET-2/6/7/8`,
   `REQ-OPS-1/2/4`, `REQ-PUBLIC-1/4`, `REQ-REACH-2/3`, `REQ-RT-2/3`, `REQ-XAI-1/3`). They are left alone
   because no §7 condition cites a non-existent id for them — §7's every `AC-*` reference now resolves to
   a definition — and writing acceptance criteria for the M2/M4/M5 areas means deciding targets that §12
   reserves to the operator. Numbering hygiene stops where a decision would have to be invented.
8. **`AC-INFRA-2`'s queue clause is satisfied by the Postgres broker, with Redis named and not built.** The

   the concurrency it deploys with.** Two replicas plus two workers is four processes appending
   `audit_events`, and `AuditLedger.append()` reads the chain head before inserting a row that points at

  executed atomically on the server: it reads the **server's** clock (`redis.call('TIME')`), refills from

  **`role`** answers *which endpoints* a credential may call; **`organisation`** answers *whose data* it may

  sentinel `organisation` value, which would have made a tenant name a capability — and a newsroom that
  registered an organisation called `*` would have owned the deployment. The matrix therefore mints a key
  whose `organisation` **is** that magic string and asserts it receives nothing (`404` on another tenant's
  job, `403` on the control plane, `201` when creating its own webhook). The control plane stays
  platform-level in v1; there is deliberately **no per-organisation admin**, and `docs/api.md` says so.

  (1) `POST /jobs/{id}/reanalyze` had **no tenancy check** — a write into another tenant, and the only job

### 6.7 Status after T46 (`REQ-INFRA-5` / `AC-INFRA-5` — a TTL that can be pinned, previewed and replayed), 2026-09-26

Landed and verified; commands and every printed count in README §2.5 and [`TODO.md`](../TODO.md) T46.

* **The criterion is three claims, so the module is three steps.** `AC-INFRA-5` asks for a sweep, for a pin
  that blocks it *and is itself recorded in the ledger*, and for idempotence. `synthverify/retention.py`
  splits along those seams: `plan_sweep()` decides and touches nothing, `apply_sweep()` deletes the planned
  rows and appends the `retention.swept` ledger entry (the caller commits), `remove_storage()` carries out
  the byte removals **after** that commit, records what actually went, and lets a store that refuses raise
  — so the failure reaches a metric and an alert instead of a 200. Migration `0006` adds the two
  tables the criterion implies — `retention_policies` (the per-org TTL) and `legal_holds` (the pin).
* **Opt-in is a row, not a number.** No `retention_policies` row means the organisation is skipped entirely,
  which is a different statement from `media_ttl_days = 0`. The failure this refuses is the common one: an
  evidence store emptied because a configuration file was missing. TTL is therefore *data* — per
  organisation, auditable, and set through the same admin API everything else policy-shaped uses.
  §4.3 names the mechanism and deliberately never sets a number, so `SV_RETENTION_DEFAULT_DAYS` ships
  **unset**, and the one global escape hatch that does exist is validated: a TTL below a whole day is a
  purge rather than a retention policy and is refused, and `0` — which reads as "keep nothing" — is refused
  for the louder reason that no configuration value should be able to make that claim implicitly. The
  scheduler has a kill switch (`SV_RETENTION_SWEEP_ENABLED`, on by default because it is inert while no
  policy row exists), and `SV_RETENTION_BATCH_LIMIT` bounds one transaction's write lock so a first sweep
  over an old install converges over passes instead of holding everything at once.
* **Content addressing turns deletion into a reference count.** Once `AC-IDAM-3` made dedup
  per-organisation, two tenants' rows can name one object — same digest, same filename, one key — and an
  ingest's artifact files are named `<digest[:12]>_<detector>_<artifact>`, so every surviving job of that
  digest shares them. A key is removable only when no row anywhere still names it. Two of the thirteen
  mutations below are precisely that mistake (`shared-object`, `shared-artifact`), and each is caught by a
  *cross-tenant* case rather than a single-tenant one — the leak is invisible to a one-org suite.
* **Order is the only irreversible choice.** Rows plus the ledger commit **first**; bytes go after. The
  reverse leaves a row pointing at evidence that no longer exists, which is a silent lie about the store.
  This ordering's worst case is an unreachable object, and the ledger row carries the planned key list so
  that state is reconcilable rather than merely invisible. The same split is why `SweepReport` has two count
  dicts: `counts` is what the pass **decided** — all an in-transaction ledger can honestly hold — and
  `outcomes` is what storage **did** (`removed` vs `absent`, `removed` vs `kept`: "we deleted it" and "it
  was not there" are different statements about forensic evidence, and the second is what a repeat pass over
  the same keys looks like).
* **Three doors, one plan.** `POST /api/v1/admin/retention/sweep` (platform admin, `dry_run` defaults to
  **true**), `synthverify retention-sweep` (previews unless `--apply`), and the `WorkerFleet` scheduler
  thread all call `sweep_once()`; `apply_sweep(dry_run=True)` returns before it touches a session, so a
  preview cannot delete by oversight and "what would this delete" cannot drift from "what did this delete".
  All seven new routes went into the `AC-IDAM-3` tenancy table — not by being asked, but because that
  matrix's completeness gate fails when an exposed operation has no rule, which moved it 41 → 48 cases and
  its `control-plane-open` mutation from 21 to 28 catching failures.
* **The scheduler's contract is four properties, each with a fake it refuses.** It **sleeps before its first
  pass**, so restarting a fleet cannot purge a tenant the moment it comes back up; it survives a failed pass
  (one store hiccup must not end TTL enforcement for every tenant, quietly and forever); every failure is
  **counted on the scrape**, because a counter nobody can page on is a log line; and the configured interval
  is a **floor, not a promise** — `max(1.0, …)`, so a mistyped `0` cannot spin a thread that takes a write
  lock and deletes evidence. `docker/prometheus-alerts.yml`'s 14th rule,
  `SynthVerifyRetentionSweepFailing`, pages on that counter (13 rules → 14; the declared-metric count 16 → 20

### 6.8 Status after T47 (`REQ-IDAM-4` / `AC-IDAM-4` — a seal is a fixed point, and the criterion turned out to be about memory), 2026-09-27

Landed and verified; commands and every printed count in README §2.5 and [`TODO.md`](../TODO.md) T47.

* **The criterion's two halves pull against each other, so the first decision was semantic.** `AC-IDAM-4`
  reads *"Benchmark proves chain verify of 1 M events < 60 s single-threaded, and a checkpoint-insertion test
  detects tampering inside a checkpointed range"*, and `REQ-IDAM-4` requires the verification to complete on
  ≥ 1 M events *"with a checkpoint scheme, still verifying every hash between checkpoints"*. Sealing every N
  rows is what makes a long walk resumable, and "still verifying every hash" forbids the thing that makes
  resumability pay. So a seal here is a **fixed point to compare against, never a licence to skip**:
  `verify()` hashes every row, cross-checks each seal as it passes it, and reports `entries_checked` beside
  `checkpoints_checked`, which makes "did the scheme let you skip work?" answerable from the response instead
  of from the source. The test that carries that clause counts rows hashed rather than trusting the walk
  (`test_every_row_is_hashed_even_when_ranges_are_sealed`), and the benchmark re-asserts
  `entries_checked == 1,000,000` on the criterion's own ledger.
* **What a seal records, and what the walk does with it.** `audit_checkpoints` (migration `0007`: seven
  columns, an index on `chain_hash`, and **no rows written by the revision** — sealing an existing ledger is
  a deliberate act, not a side effect of `upgrade`) holds `seq`, `prev_seq`, the `head_hash` the range
  reaches, **`events_in_range`**, `created_at`, `prev_chain_hash` and its own `chain_hash`, so the seals chain
  over themselves the way the events do. One lands inside the same transaction as the append that closes its
  range, every `SV_AUDIT_CHECKPOINT_EVERY` (5 000) events, opt-out at `0`. Verification then fires four checks
  at each seal — the seal's head against the chain's, the seal's predecessor against the previous seal, the
  sealed record against its own digest, the recorded range size against the walk's count — plus a post-loop
  guard that no seal points past the end of the ledger. The **recorded range size is the invariant a
  re-sealing forger cannot satisfy**, and the ordering is chosen for diagnosis: `seal-chain-unchecked` still
  earns its place even though the count fires next, because without it the report blames the *events* and
  sends an operator to the wrong table.
* **The time clause was already met by v1, so what this tranche fixed is memory.** Measured on this machine,
  one phase per child process so each peak is attributable, both read shapes on every run: the shipped walk
  verifies 1 000 000 chained events in **11.38 s at 65.5 MiB** on SQLite (+5.5 MiB over that process's
  60.0 MiB idle floor) and **16.40 s at 69.3 MiB** on `postgres:16` 16.15 (+3.5 over 65.8). The read it
  replaces — `select(AuditEvent).scalars().all()`, then hash every object — took **17.72 s at 2 392 MiB** and
  **23.18 s at 2 462 MiB**. An earlier pair of runs measured 7.40 s / 13.67 s shipped and 10.80 s / 18.35 s
  legacy, so the *seconds* are reported as a spread that tracks machine load, and the load-independent finding
  is the ratio the benchmark clauses on: materialising must allocate more than 8x the streaming read's
  overhead over the floor, and it measured **424x** on SQLite and **685x** on PostgreSQL. `GET
  /admin/audit/verify` is the named surface in `REQ-IDAM-4`, and it streams ten columns a page at a time
  through a server-side cursor (`stream_results` + `yield_per(1000)`) because an admin endpoint that allocates
  proportional to the ledger's size is a denial of service an auditor hands themselves.
* **The gate is a benchmark, because the criterion is a benchmark.** `scripts/ledger_bench.py` runs the
  criterion itself and prints every phase, then drives four tampering probes into that same 1 M-row ledger and
  restores the rows each one breaks (`restore kept every row (1,000,000 of 1,000,000)` on all four):
  `mid-edit` → `entry hash mismatch at seq=500000`, `relinked` → caught by the seal closing the range,
  `deleted-resealed` → caught by the recorded range size, and `relinked-resealed` → **`verified=True`**, which
  the run *requires*, because that is the documented privileged-rewrite boundary and the alternative to
  asserting it is prose that drifts better than the code. Alongside it: **36** cases in
  `tests/test_audit_checkpoints.py`, passing with **0 skips on both dialects** (5.3 s SQLite / 62.3 s
  Postgres), and `make ledger-mutations` removing eleven enforcements one at a time — all eleven caught, out
  of a **35-case** baseline that runs clean, with `--check-anchors` reporting **11/11** anchors quoting exactly
  one place in the source. Two witnesses beyond the suite: fed a build with the cursor disabled, **the
  benchmark itself fails** (at 50 000 events, `RESULT: FAIL (1 clause(s) not met)`, exit 1), and
  `seal-chain-unchecked` is the mode where a later check still catches the forgery but names the wrong table.
* **A defect the criterion does not ask about, found by running the gate: the walk held the event loop.**
  Making 1 M events *measurable* produced this app's first route that can run for tens of seconds, and the
  handler was `async def` over a synchronous session. Measured, not inferred — with a stub that sleeps 2.0 s in
  place of the walk, a concurrent `GET /healthz` (no database at all) returned after **1.63 s**, so the
  replica was not unhealthy, it was unable to say it was healthy. **Fixed as `TODO.md` T51, in the release
  pass, and as a class rather than a route**: grepping the package found no `asyncio` call at all and 40
  `async def` handlers with four `await` sites between them, so the seven endpoints whose work is bounded by

## 8. Explicitly out of scope

`OUT-1` **Hosted commercial detection APIs** (cloud moderation/deepfake endpoints) as a required
component. They fail FC-2/FC-5 on the verdict path. Adapters MAY exist as opt-in plugins.

`OUT-2` **Paywalled capability or a crippled free tier.** FC-9 forbids degrading the *model* for free
users; only routing authority is reduced.

`OUT-3` **Legal admissibility claims.** Reports are explainable evidence, not certified expert
findings; no court-adherence certification is promised. Narratives already say "statistical indicator,
not proof" and that framing stays.

`OUT-4` **Non-permissive weights/datasets.** Even when they are the accuracy SOTA. If a better model
fails FC-3, it is replaced, not licensed "for now".

`OUT-5` **Paid SaaS for convenience** (managed queues, metered APM, hosted Postgres as a hard
dependency) on the verdict path — permitted as operator choice, never required.

`OUT-6` **Mass surveillance or identity-at-scale features.** The product verifies a *media item* a user
already possesses; no face-recognition watchlist, no person tracking. This is a values line, tied to
SDG-16 framing in `ba-context.md` §5.

`OUT-7` **Real-time video streaming** in v2 — audio only (REQ-RT-*); video stays batch/async.
